Benchmark results

English is one of the 29 languages in the KuralHub benchmark. Each of the 11 frozen speech models was trained with a small classification head on the shared emotions and scored on a held-out test split. Over all 22 runs the mean test accuracy was 51.0% (median 57.0%). The best run reached 82.0% with hubert-base-ls960. Compare with other languages.

IEMOCAP is a multimodal, multispeaker dataset containing ~12 hours of audiovisual data, including speech, video, facial motion capture, and text transcriptions. It features dyadic sessions with scripted and improvised interactions designed to elicit emotions. The dataset is annotated with categorical (anger, happiness, sadness, neutrality) and dimensional (valence, activation, dominance) labels, making it valuable for studying expressive human communication.

The Indian Emotional Speech Corpora (IESC) is a dataset containing 600 emotional speech recordings from 8 North Indian speakers (5 males, 3 females). The audio files, recorded in a noise-free environment using a mobile phone and headset, cover five emotions: neutral, happy, angry, sad, and fearful. Each .wav file follows a structured naming convention indicating emotion, repetition, speaker, and sentence ID.

Used in the KuralHub benchmark (run ID IESC). See the results above.

The RAVDESS (Ryerson Audio-Visual Database of Emotional Speech and Song) is a dataset containing 1440 speech-only audio files (16-bit, 48kHz WAV) recorded by 24 professional actors (12 female, 12 male). The dataset includes seven emotions—calm, happy, sad, angry, fearful, surprise, and disgust—each performed at two intensity levels (normal and strong), along with a neutral expression. The recordings feature two lexically-matched statements in a neutral North American accent. The full dataset, including speech and song, is available on Zenodo (24.8 GB).

Used in the KuralHub benchmark (run ID RAVDESS). See the results above.
