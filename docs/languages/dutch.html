Paper
Development of vocal emotion recognition in school-age children: The EmoHI test for hearing-impaired populations
Download
Request
Contact
mail

The dataset comprises recordings from six native Dutch speakers (three male, three female) who articulated two pseudospeech sentences—"Koun se mina lod belam" and "Nekal ibam soud molen"—expressing three core emotions: happy, sad, and angry. These sentences are non-meaningful in Dutch, English, or any other Indo-European languages, ensuring a focus on emotional prosody without semantic influence. Recordings were conducted in an anechoic chamber at a 44.1 kHz sampling rate.

An online survey with Dutch and English adults was utilized to evaluate the recognizability of these emotional expressions, leading to the selection of four speakers whose renditions were most accurately identified. The final stimulus set includes 36 experimental stimuli, with each speaker contributing three productions per emotion, and an additional four practice stimuli for training purposes.

Participants consisted of 58 Dutch and 25 English children aged 4 to 12, and 15 Dutch and 15 English adults aged 20 to 30, all with normal hearing and monolingual language backgrounds. Additionally, preliminary data were collected from 14 prelingually deaf Dutch children with cochlear implants aged 4 to 16. Normal hearing participants underwent pure-tone audiometry to confirm hearing thresholds at 20 dB HL across octave frequencies between 500 and 4,000 Hz. Written informed consent was obtained from adult participants and the parents or legal guardians of child participants prior to data collection.

Testing was conducted in quiet environments: normal hearing children were assessed at their homes, while adults were tested in university settings. The experiment commenced with a training session of four practice stimuli, followed by 36 experimental stimuli presented in a randomized order, lasting approximately 6 to 8 minutes. Stimuli were delivered via Sennheiser HD 380 Pro headphones for normal hearing participants and Logitech Z200 loudspeakers for cochlear implant children, calibrated to 65 dBA. Cochlear implant children used their regular device settings consistently throughout the experiment. Participants responded by selecting one of three clowns on a touchscreen interface corresponding to the perceived emotion, receiving visual feedback—confetti for correct responses and a head-shaking parrot for incorrect ones—to maintain engagement and indicate progress.
